About

A better question is worth more than a faster answer.

ParsisPress exists because the hardest part of starting a company is not building. It is deciding what is worth building — and that decision is usually made on evidence thinner than anyone acknowledges.

Mission

Make opportunity research rigorous instead of reassuring.

Most tools in this category generate ideas. Generation is now cheap, which means it is no longer useful — anyone can produce two hundred startup concepts before lunch, and almost none of them survive contact with a customer.

ParsisPress takes the opposite position. The product is organised around the parts of discovery that resist automation: deciding whether a problem is severe, whether the customer is reachable, whether an alternative already exists, and what evidence would change your mind.

That means the platform’s job is to make a decision easier to defend. Every brief states what is known, what is inferred, and what has simply been assumed. Every score is accompanied by its dimensions. Every validation plan is designed to be capable of ending the project it belongs to.

A founder who spends a fortnight finding out that an idea does not work has saved a year. That outcome is a success for the product, and it is the one we optimise for.

Operating principles

Four commitments the product has to honour.

These are constraints, not values statements. Each one rules out something the product could easily do and chose not to.

  • 01

    Problems before products

    No brief is written before the customer and the moment are. If a workflow cannot be described precisely enough to interview about, it is not ready to evaluate.

  • 02

    Depth over volume

    A smaller set examined properly is worth more than a large list. The product is built to help a founder drop an idea, which is the outcome volume tools avoid producing.

  • 03

    Hypotheses, labelled as hypotheses

    Generated content is marked as generated. Claims are separated into what is known, what is inferred, and what is assumed, so nobody mistakes a plausible sentence for evidence.

  • 04

    The founder decides

    AI organises material and applies a consistent rubric. It does not validate a market, interview a customer, or predict whether a business will work.

Honest scope

What this product does not claim.

An early-stage product that overstates its evidence is harder to evaluate and impossible to build on. So the limits are stated as plainly as the features.

  • NotThat any opportunity described here exists as a business
  • NotThat any score predicts commercial success
  • NotThat the platform has customers, partnerships, or revenue
  • NotThat market data has been verified or sourced
  • NotThat using the platform guarantees a better outcome
