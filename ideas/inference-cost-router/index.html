Model Routing Under Cost Pressure

Routing infrastructure that sends each request to the cheapest model that satisfies its quality requirement, with quality enforced rather than assumed.

Illustrative opportunity score

67 / 100

Demo heuristic

Savings math is attractive and defensible with the right evidence, but vendor price convergence is a structural headwind.

The problem

Teams either send everything to a frontier model and overpay, or route on price alone and quietly degrade quality in ways customers notice later.

Executive summary

Model Routing Under Cost Pressure is a platform concept for engineering teams with meaningful inference volume in production. It addresses a problem that recurs often enough to be felt in operating cost rather than noticed as an inconvenience, and it becomes buildable because recent capability changes made the underlying work tractable.

The first version would be deliberately narrow — one workflow, one countable unit — with 4-6 weeks of work needed before any code is written to establish whether the problem is real. The illustrative score of 67/100 reflects medium confidence in the read, with the reasoning broken out dimension by dimension below.

Target customer profile

A team running 50 million-plus requests a month across several models, with a monthly bill large enough to attract attention and no mechanism to prove that a cheaper model handled a given request acceptably.

Existing alternatives and competitor categories

  • Single-model stacks
  • Vendor-provided routing tiers
  • Hand-tuned per-endpoint model choices
  • Manual monthly bill analysis

Most of these are adjacent rather than direct. That usually means the budget exists, the buyer tolerates the current state, and the real competitor is doing nothing at all.

Why the opportunity might matter now

Routing logic historically required offline labels and a rebuild cycle. Online routing against per-request quality signal became viable as evaluation and judge models got cheap enough to run inline, which is what makes cost routing defensible rather than a gamble.

Proposed solution

A routing layer that evaluates each request against its requirement in real time, routes to the cheapest sufficient model, and provides the record needed to defend the decision afterwards.

Potential business model

Usage-based pricing on requests routed, taking a small share of the savings so the incentive aligns.

MVP scope

  1. 01Per-request routing decision with explicit quality requirement
  2. 02Fallback behaviour when the cheap path fails the requirement
  3. 03Spend and quality reporting split by route
  4. 04Per-team controls over what may be downgraded
  5. 05Record of routing decisions for later audit

The scope above is deliberately one workflow with a countable unit. Anything broader belongs in the second version, if the first version works.

Key assumptions

  1. 01Customers would rather have a defensible quality record than the cheapest route
  2. 02Online evaluation cost stays small relative to inference savings
  3. 03Teams can express quality requirements per request type
  4. 04Savings are large enough to justify routing infrastructure

Each of these is a claim that could turn out to be false. They are listed so a founder knows what to attack first.

Risks and unknowns

  1. 01Model vendors aggressively close the price-quality gap
  2. 02Cheap routing can fail silently and expensively
  3. 03Infrastructure teams resist another layer in the request path
  4. 04Requires meaningful volume to be worth operating

Validation plan

  1. 01Analyse a real bill and compute achievable savings per request class
  2. 02Backtest routing decisions against historical quality labels
  3. 03Shadow-run the router in production without taking effect, and compare
  4. 04Ask what would make teams distrust an automatic downgrade

Run this before writing code. If the interviews come back without a recurring incident, the honest outcome is to drop the idea rather than reframe it.

Three customer interview questions

  1. 1Who looks at your inference bill, and what decision do they make from it?
  2. 2How do you currently decide which model handles which endpoint?
  3. 3What would a wrong downgrade cost you relative to a higher bill?

Suggested first experiments

  1. 01Backtest a routing policy on historical traffic and compute real savings
  2. 02Shadow deployment reporting where it would have routed differently
  3. 03Manual routing policy as a service for one team
