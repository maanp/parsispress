Treasury Document Reconciliation

Reconciliation software for finance teams that extracts terms, obligations, and mismatches from counterparty documents and flags the ones that need a human.

Illustrative opportunity score

66 / 100

Demo heuristic

Quantifiable savings with a clear buyer, competing against AP suites that already hold the documents.

The problem

Finance teams discover billing errors, duplicate obligations, and non-standard terms only after a payment goes out or a quarter closes.

Executive summary

Treasury Document Reconciliation is a product concept for finance operations and treasury teams in mid-market companies. It addresses a problem that recurs often enough to be felt in operating cost rather than noticed as an inconvenience, and it becomes buildable because recent capability changes made the underlying work tractable.

The first version would be deliberately narrow — one workflow, one countable unit — with 4-5 weeks of work needed before any code is written to establish whether the problem is real. The illustrative score of 66/100 reflects medium confidence in the read, with the reasoning broken out dimension by dimension below.

Target customer profile

A 100-1,000 person business with a small finance team handling vendor contracts, invoices, and payment runs in email and shared drives. Month-end is a manual hunt for mismatches. Nobody outside finance can answer whether a contract auto-renews.

Existing alternatives and competitor categories

  • Manual document review
  • Accounts payable automation suites
  • Contract lifecycle management systems
  • Shared drives and email

Most of these are adjacent rather than direct. That usually means the budget exists, the buyer tolerates the current state, and the real competitor is doing nothing at all.

Why the opportunity might matter now

Document extraction became reliable enough for mixed real-world contracts, and the bottleneck moved to prioritisation: which discrepancies are large enough to matter. That prioritisation is a language task over a document corpus that used to be unreadable at scale.

Proposed solution

A reconciliation inbox that extracts terms and amounts from every incoming document, compares them against contracts and payment history, and surfaces only the discrepancies with financial consequence.

Potential business model

Per-entity subscription with volume-based document pricing, positioned against the cost of late detection.

MVP scope

  1. 01Document ingestion from the existing shared inbox
  2. 02Extraction of amounts, dates, and renewal terms
  3. 03Comparison against contract and payment history
  4. 04Ranked discrepancy queue by financial consequence
  5. 05Approval path before anything reaches a payment run

The scope above is deliberately one workflow with a countable unit. Anything broader belongs in the second version, if the first version works.

Key assumptions

  1. 01Extraction accuracy on real contracts is high enough to avoid rework
  2. 02Ranking by consequence is more useful than surfacing everything
  3. 03Finance teams will adopt a tool that changes close-week routines
  4. 04Contract data is accessible without a CLM migration

Each of these is a claim that could turn out to be false. They are listed so a founder knows what to attack first.

Risks and unknowns

  1. 01AP automation vendors already own document intake
  2. 02Mid-market finance teams are conservative about new tooling
  3. 03Ranking errors destroy trust faster than surfacing nothing
  4. 04Category funding is concentrated in adjacent accounting products

Validation plan

  1. 01Have a finance manager reconstruct the last month-end and time each step
  2. 02Run extraction on 200 real documents and measure discrepancy recall
  3. 03Ask what a ranked discrepancy list would have caught that they did not catch
  4. 04Compare against the cost of one missed duplicate payment

Run this before writing code. If the interviews come back without a recurring incident, the honest outcome is to drop the idea rather than reframe it.

Three customer interview questions

  1. 1What was the most expensive mistake your team caught late in the last year?
  2. 2How long does month-end take, and which step is the least defensible?
  3. 3What would you do differently if you saw renewal terms and mismatches before payment ran?

Suggested first experiments

  1. 01Manual discrepancy review service for one finance team
  2. 02Extraction benchmark on a real document corpus
  3. 03Ranked-digest email prototype before building an interface
