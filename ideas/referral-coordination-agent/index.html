Referral Coordination Layer

A coordination layer between specialists and referring practices that closes the loop on referrals instead of leaving them in a fax queue.

Illustrative opportunity score

58 / 100

Demo heuristic

Real operational waste with a clear owner on one side, but the value only exists if capacity and staffing problems are also solved.

The problem

Patients wait for specialist appointments while referrals stall in inboxes and faxes, and nobody in the system owns the handoff well enough to report on it.

Executive summary

Referral Coordination Layer is a product concept for specialist clinics and multi-site primary care groups. It addresses a problem that recurs often enough to be felt in operating cost rather than noticed as an inconvenience, and it becomes buildable because recent capability changes made the underlying work tractable.

The first version would be deliberately narrow — one workflow, one countable unit — with 4-6 weeks of work needed before any code is written to establish whether the problem is real. The illustrative score of 58/100 reflects low confidence in the read, with the reasoning broken out dimension by dimension below.

Target customer profile

A specialty clinic with referral volume it cannot absorb and a relationship with primary care groups it has never formalised. Referrals arrive by fax, portal message, and phone call. A coordinator chases them manually and reports wait times from memory.

Existing alternatives and competitor categories

  • Fax and inbox coordination
  • EHR referral modules
  • Patient portals and self-scheduling
  • Outsourced referral services

Most of these are adjacent rather than direct. That usually means the budget exists, the buyer tolerates the current state, and the real competitor is doing nothing at all.

Why the opportunity might matter now

Closing the referral loop is mostly a structured communication problem that was previously blocked by unusable interoperability. Language models can now reconcile fax, portal, and phone-derived intake into a single tracked state without demanding an integration project from either side.

Proposed solution

A shared coordination layer that ingests referrals in whatever format they arrive, extracts clinical and administrative detail, tracks status against the receiving clinic's actual capacity, and reports true wait times to referring providers.

Potential business model

Per-referral fee paid by the receiving clinic, with a reporting tier for referring groups. Per-referral pricing aligns with the volume that actually matters.

MVP scope

  1. 01Ingestion from fax, email, and portal message
  2. 02Extraction of referral detail with confidence flagging
  3. 03Status tracking against clinic capacity rather than a fixed queue
  4. 04Referring-provider visibility without a full EHR integration
  5. 05Wait-time reporting that reflects real state, not estimates

The scope above is deliberately one workflow with a countable unit. Anything broader belongs in the second version, if the first version works.

Key assumptions

  1. 01Receiving clinics will pay per referral for faster throughput
  2. 02Extraction accuracy is sufficient to avoid manual correction
  3. 03Referring providers will accept status visibility without an integration
  4. 04Referral volume is high enough per clinic to matter

Each of these is a claim that could turn out to be false. They are listed so a founder knows what to attack first.

Risks and unknowns

  1. 01Patient data handling and consent flows
  2. 02Fax does not die on the timeline a product needs
  3. 03Incumbent EHR vendors could add exactly this
  4. 04Coordination improvements can simply become deeper intake queues

Validation plan

  1. 01Count how many referrals per month a typical specialty clinic chases manually
  2. 02Instrument one clinic's referral flow for a month and measure true closure rate
  3. 03Test whether a coordinator would rather have faster status or fewer inappropriate referrals
  4. 04Validate per-referral pricing against the cost of one missed referral

Run this before writing code. If the interviews come back without a recurring incident, the honest outcome is to drop the idea rather than reframe it.

Three customer interview questions

  1. 1How many referrals does your team chase by phone each week, and what happens to the ones you cannot reach?
  2. 2What do you tell a patient today when you do not know where their referral is?
  3. 3Would you pay to shorten the referral wait, or to reduce the number of referrals you receive?

Suggested first experiments

  1. 01Manual coordination service for one clinic, measuring closure rate
  2. 02Fax-to-structured extraction prototype on 200 real referrals
  3. 03Shadow a referral coordinator to find the real bottleneck before building
