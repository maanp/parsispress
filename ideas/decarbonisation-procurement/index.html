Decarbonisation Procurement Brief

A supplier-sourcing tool that identifies which emissions reduction is cheapest to buy for a given operating site, before procurement has committed budget.

Illustrative opportunity score

55 / 100

Demo heuristic

Attractive economics if the data holds up, but site-level data quality is an unproven dependency.

The problem

Operations teams know they need to reduce emissions but cannot rank supplier interventions, so they buy whatever is easiest to contract rather than what actually moves the number.

Executive summary

Decarbonisation Procurement Brief is a platform concept for sustainability and procurement teams in industrial and logistics operations. It addresses a problem that recurs often enough to be felt in operating cost rather than noticed as an inconvenience, and it becomes buildable because recent capability changes made the underlying work tractable.

The first version would be deliberately narrow — one workflow, one countable unit — with 6-10 weeks of work needed before any code is written to establish whether the problem is real. The illustrative score of 55/100 reflects low confidence in the read, with the reasoning broken out dimension by dimension below.

Target customer profile

An operator with an emissions target and no engineering capacity to model reduction options. Supplier conversations are relationships, not analysis. Each abatement business case takes weeks to build and the answer is a guess with a spreadsheet attached.

Existing alternatives and competitor categories

  • Consultant-built abatement studies
  • Internal analyst spreadsheets
  • Supplier-proposed programmes
  • Generic emissions calculators

Most of these are adjacent rather than direct. That usually means the budget exists, the buyer tolerates the current state, and the real competitor is doing nothing at all.

Why the opportunity might matter now

Abatement analysis has always been feasible but slow, which meant it happened once a year with a consultant. Continuous analysis became possible when supplier data, equipment specifications, and market prices could be reasoned over in combination rather than manually assembled.

Proposed solution

A continuously updated ranking of available reduction interventions per site, each with estimated cost, volume, and evidence requirements, so procurement can act on a shortlist instead of a study.

Potential business model

Per-site subscription with a services tier for the initial data build, since supplier data quality is the real cost centre.

MVP scope

  1. 01Site-level emissions inventory with explicit data provenance
  2. 02Intervention shortlist ranked by cost per unit abated
  3. 03Supplier matching based on actual available equipment
  4. 04Evidence requirements listed per intervention
  5. 05Progress tracked against target rather than reported annually

The scope above is deliberately one workflow with a countable unit. Anything broader belongs in the second version, if the first version works.

Key assumptions

  1. 01Site-level data quality is sufficient to rank interventions
  2. 02Procurement uses a shortlist rather than a deep-dive study
  3. 03Supplier options are real and available in the operator's region
  4. 04Emissions targets create budget that exists independent of reporting

Each of these is a claim that could turn out to be false. They are listed so a founder knows what to attack first.

Risks and unknowns

  1. 01Data quality at site level can make ranking meaningless
  2. 02Concentrated industries are conservative about disclosing operations
  3. 03Equipment suppliers sell their own solutions
  4. 04Value depends on carbon prices and mandates, which are political

Validation plan

  1. 01Ask operators which intervention they would fund first and why
  2. 02Build one site's shortlist manually with an analyst and compare against the consultant study
  3. 03Check whether the ranked shortlist changes what they actually purchase
  4. 04Validate that supplier availability is real rather than assumed

Run this before writing code. If the interviews come back without a recurring incident, the honest outcome is to drop the idea rather than reframe it.

Three customer interview questions

  1. 1Which reduction have you bought or avoided in the last two years, and how was it chosen?
  2. 2What information would you need to commit budget to an unfamiliar reduction?
  3. 3Who currently builds your abatement business cases?

Suggested first experiments

  1. 01Manual shortlist for one site, compared to its consultant study
  2. 02Interview procurement about how they choose suppliers today
  3. 03Test the ranking against what operators say they would fund
