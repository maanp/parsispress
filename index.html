The problem

The best opportunities rarely announce themselves.

They sit inside a workflow someone has stopped questioning. They belong to an industry too small for the vendors. They are obvious only to the person who does the work every week.

Finding them requires reading how businesses actually operate — not summarising what already happened to be funded.

  • 01

    Discovering problems, not chasing hype

    Idea generators produce plausible-sounding concepts with no customer attached. The hard part is finding a specific person, in a specific recurring situation, who already spends money or time solving it.

  • 02

    Finding underserved customers

    Most industries are served by software designed for buyers ten times their size. Finding the segment that was skipped requires reading workflows rather than market reports.

  • 03

    Turning research into a testable concept

    Signals, interviews, and market context arrive as separate threads. A founder needs them assembled into one hypothesis with a validation plan attached, not a folder of links.

The ParsisPress approach

From signal to startup.

A structured path from something that changed in the world to something you can test with a customer this month.

  1. 01Step

    Discover signals

    Explore emerging technologies, industry shifts, and recurring customer pain points. Each signal is framed as a hypothesis with a named source and a cheap test that could disprove it.

    • Technology and cost thresholds crossed
    • New record-keeping obligations
    • Workflows people have stopped questioning
  2. 02Step

    Evaluate opportunities

    Assess problem severity, frequency, willingness to pay, customer accessibility, competitive alternatives, and technical feasibility — then state what would invalidate the read.

    • Six-dimension directional scoring
    • Explicit assumptions and unknowns
    • Competitive alternative mapping
  3. 03Step

    Build with conviction

    Turn a selected idea into a hypothesis, a two-week validation plan, customer interview questions, and a scoped first version you could put in front of someone.

    • Two-week validation plans
    • Three customer questions per brief
    • MVP scope limited to one workflow

Where AI helps. AI organises and analyses large volumes of material — documents, workflows, alternatives, constraints — so a founder spends time on judgement rather than collection. Where it does not. It cannot tell you whether a problem is real. Only a customer can, and validating that remains the founder’s responsibility.

Export Compliance Copilot

An AI compliance assistant for small exporters that turns scattered product, origin, and material data into classification answers and document packs.

Customer
SMB exporters in apparel, home goods, and specialty food
Problem
Small exporters cannot justify a full-time compliance function, so every shipment decision is made by guesswork or by copying last year's answer.

Why now

Cross-border tariff schedules have become volatile enough that last year's classification is frequently wrong, while the operational cost of being wrong — demurrage, storage, penalties — stays constant. At the same time, multimodal models can now read a supplier invoice, a material spec sheet, and a product photo well enough to propose a classification a human can approve. The bottleneck moved from knowledge to judgement.

Voice-First Field Operations

Voice-first operations software for field teams that captures work notes, parts usage, and handover context hands-free while the technician is already covered in grease.

Customer
Maintenance and field service teams at multi-site industrial operators
Problem
Field technicians lose billable hours writing notes after the fact, and the operational knowledge that would let a site run better disappears with each handover.

Why now

Speech models now handle noisy, jargon-heavy industrial environments at a usable accuracy, including vocabulary specific to a single site. That unlocks an interface the industry has always wanted and never had: talk while you work. The second shift is data capture, because voice notes are only useful if they become structured history rather than an audio file nobody reopens.

Outpatient clinics and specialist practices

Opportunity intelligence

Every score shows its working.

ParsisPress does not publish a single number without the dimensions behind it. A founder should be able to disagree with the assessment and still act on it.

  • Scores are directional heuristics, not predictions of success.
  • Each dimension states what it measures and why it scored that way.
  • Assumptions and unknowns are listed separately from the read.
  • No market-size figures are shown, because they invite lazy reasoning.

Why ParsisPress

Depth over volume.

A thousand generated ideas help nobody decide anything. The goal is a smaller number of opportunities examined closely enough that a founder knows which one to spend a fortnight on — and which to drop.

ParsisPress is built to make better-informed decisions, not to promise that any particular idea will succeed. Nobody can promise that, and a product implying otherwise is selling optimism instead of analysis.

  • 01

    Problems before products

    No opportunity is described before the customer and the moment are. If the workflow cannot be described precisely, it is not ready to evaluate.

  • 02

    Evidence before hype

    Claims are labelled as verified, inferred, or hypothesised. A funding wave is treated as context, never as a reason.

  • 03

    Specific customers before broad markets

    Every brief names a population you could list. Market size is deliberately absent, because it invites reasoning backwards from a desire to build.

  • 04

    Validation before building

    The first deliverable of every opportunity is a plan that can end it, not a roadmap that defends it.

Who it is for

Built for the moment before you build.

The same framework, read differently depending on where you are in the process.

  • 01

    First-time founders

    You need a starting point that is specific enough to act on. The explorer gives you a named customer, a repeated workflow, and the questions to ask in week one.

  • 02

    Technical founders

    You can build the thing. What you lack is a commercially meaningful problem. Briefs are structured so the technical constraint sits beside the buying decision.

  • 03

    Indie hackers

    You are looking for a small, focused product that pays for itself. The framework rewards narrow scope, short validation cycles, and an obvious buyer.

  • 04

    Operators

    You see the waste but not the product. Every brief separates what automation can remove from what still needs a person, which is usually the useful part.

  • 05

    Investors and researchers

    You need to map where categories are forming without reading everything. The evaluation structure makes gaps and unknowns visible instead of hiding them behind a total.
